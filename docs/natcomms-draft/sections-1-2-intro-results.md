# Transparency without pricing: a credit-level forensic account of collapse in the first tokenized carbon pool

## Introduction

Carbon markets are central to global climate policy, channelling billions of dollars toward emission reduction projects worldwide. Yet these markets function only if participants can distinguish high-quality credits (those representing real, additional emission reductions) from low-quality ones. When they cannot, or when market design fails to price quality differences, the resulting adverse selection can undermine an entire market segment^5,29^. The voluntary carbon market (VCM), valued at over \$2 billion annually, faces exactly this challenge: credits vary enormously in environmental integrity, and recent empirical work estimates that fewer than 16% of credits from investigated projects represent genuine emission reductions^2^, with at least 52% of Indian wind offsets allocated to non-additional projects^46^.

Blockchain-based tokenization promised to solve this problem through radical transparency. By recording every transaction on a public ledger, tokenized carbon pools would, in theory, allow any participant to verify credit quality before trading. The first and largest such experiment was the Base Carbon Tonne (BCT) pool, a public-ledger carbon pool (operated by Toucan Protocol) that accepted verified credits from the Verra registry and issued fungible units at a uniform price regardless of underlying credit quality. The pool's value collapsed from approximately \$6 per tonne in late 2021 to below \$0.50 by mid-2023. Industry commentary and academic analyses attributed this collapse to low-quality forestry credits (specifically REDD+, Reducing Emissions from Deforestation and Forest Degradation) flooding the pool^5,6,16,17,18^. This narrative drew support from the broader literature documenting REDD+ crediting problems^2,3,4^ but was never tested against the pool's actual credit-level composition data, which are publicly recorded and trivially queryable.

Here, we analyse every deposit ({{composition.n_deposits}}), every redemption ({{redemption.n_transfer_events}}), every credit token (161 individually scored, of 345 total), and every market participant ({{selection.n_wallets}} depositors, {{redemption.n_unique_redeemers}} redeemers) across BCT's entire operating history. The data contradict the prevailing narrative. BCT was {{composition.renewable_pct_tonnes}}% renewable energy credits (predominantly grid-connected hydropower, with wind and solar, registered across China, India, Turkey, and Brazil) with near-zero additionality, and just 4.2% REDD+. A small number of accounts extracted most of the high-demand credits (1.55 million tonnes), while 9.3 million tonnes of low-quality credits remain stranded to date, to our knowledge the largest documented carbon credit graveyard. A comparison of identical credits across two pools with different quality screens reveals that the same credits were 100% redeemed from BCT but only 28.5% redeemed from a quality-gated pool, a contrast consistent with pool design, rather than credit type, shaping asset fate. We term this outcome *design-enabled adverse selection*: a variant of Akerlof's (1970) lemons problem in which quality information is publicly observable but not priced, so that uniform pricing of heterogeneous assets under voluntary participation is sufficient to produce market failure. Because every transaction in this market is publicly recorded, its complete ledger supports the first credit-level forensic account of this dynamic, the kind of reconstruction that opaque off-chain carbon markets cannot supply. We treat the market as a case study of a general prediction. The implication for pooled markets broadly, including the \$10 billion tokenized real-world asset sector, is that transparency without price differentiation appears insufficient for market function.


## Results

### BCT's real composition contradicts the REDD+ narrative

We extracted all {{composition.n_deposits}} deposit records from BCT's public transaction ledger, covering {{composition.n_projects}} unique Verra VCS (Verified Carbon Standard) projects and approximately 22 million tonnes of tokenized carbon credits (see Methods for data collection).

The composition was overwhelmingly dominated by renewable energy credits (Fig. 1): {{composition.renewable_pct_tonnes}}% of total deposited tonnage consisted of grid-connected wind, hydro, and solar projects from China and India, registered between 2008 and 2013. These are precisely the CDM-era (Clean Development Mechanism, a now-defunct UN crediting programme) project categories that the ICVCM (Integrity Council for the Voluntary Carbon Market) has declined to approve for its Core Carbon Principles (CCP) label, and that multiple independent studies have documented as having near-zero additionality: Cames et al.^24^ and Schneider et al.^25^ in programme-level assessments, Probst et al.^2^ in a meta-analysis finding no statistically significant emission reductions from wind power CDM projects, and Calel et al.^46^ in a project-level study estimating 52% non-additionality among Indian wind offsets. The projects would largely have been built regardless of carbon revenue. redd_pct_tonnes}}% of the pool, more than 16 times smaller than the renewable share. Even combining all nature-based categories (REDD+, afforestation/reforestation [ARR], improved forest management [IFM]), the total is approximately 10% versus {{composition.renewable_pct_tonnes}}% for renewables alone.


### Base-rate selection: BCT over-selected renewables at 1.87$\times$ the registry rate

BCT's renewable dominance could, in principle, reflect the shape of the VCS-eligible universe. We obtained Verra VCS issuance data from MSCI (2023) and Ecosystem Marketplace (2023) indicating that renewable energy credits constitute approximately 37% of total VCS issuance by volume (sensitivity range 26--48% depending on vintage window).

BCT's renewable share ({{composition.renewable_pct_tonnes}}% by tonnage) is 1.87$\times$ the VCS base rate (selection coefficient: the ratio of BCT's renewable share to the base rate; 1 = no over-selection). Because deposits cluster by account, we use account-clustered inference rather than a naive binomial test: an account-level permutation test (10,000 iterations) produces $p$ <0.0001, and {{selection.wallet_majority_renewable_pct}}% of accounts hold majority-renewable portfolios. The over-selection is robust to conservative base-rate assumptions (remaining {{selection.conservative_selection_coeff}}$\times$ at a 48% null) and to account-level bootstrap and HHI-adjusted tests (Supplementary Methods).

Conversely, REDD+ is under-represented at {{selection.redd_underrepresentation}}$\times$ the VCS base rate (BCT {{composition.redd_pct_tonnes}}% vs. VCS $\sim$17%), directly contradicting the narrative that REDD+ credits overwhelmed the pool.

**Bridge-level decomposition.** Toucan's bridge (the mechanism that moves an off-chain credit on-chain) passed essentially every Verra-bridged credit into BCT, {{bridge_decomposition.bct_coverage_pct}}% of tokens and 99.6% of tonnage (Supplementary Methods), so the 1.87$\times$ over-selection was a bridge-level phenomenon: the permissionless (open to anyone) bridge disproportionately attracted renewable credits from Verra, rather than depositors selecting them from a diverse universe. Pool-level selection space was essentially zero.


### Price-quality feedback loop: composition predicts price collapse

We next ask whether quality degradation drove the price collapse. We merged daily BCT prices in US-dollar terms (828 observations, October 2021 -- July 2024; see Methods) with daily cumulative quality metrics from the deposit stream ($n$ = 331 overlapping days).

**Correlation.** BCT's price and cumulative Pool Quality Deficit (PQD, a tonnage-weighted 0--1 measure of how far a pool's credits fall below maximum quality; Methods) are strongly correlated (Pearson $r$ = {{price_quality.pearson_r}}, $p$ <10^-66): as the pool's quality declined, its price declined proportionally. In a first-differenced OLS regression (the credible specification for non-stationary series), each percentage-point increase in the renewable share of deposits predicts a \$1.80 decrease in BCT's price ($\beta$ = $-$1.8, $p$ <0.001).

**Redemption-side evidence.** Analysis of {{redemption.n_transfer_events}} Transfer events from the BCT pool contract reveals that the selection dynamic operated on both sides. Non-renewable credits were preferentially redeemed out of the pool (Table 4): industrial gas {{redemption.industrial_gas_redeemed_pct}}% redeemed, REDD+ {{redemption.redd_redeemed_pct}}%, IFM {{redemption.ifm_redeemed_pct}}%, ARR {{redemption.arr_redeemed_pct}}%, versus just {{redemption.renewable_redeemed_pct}}% of renewable credits. The tonnage-weighted mean composite quality score (0–100, higher = better) of redeemed credits ({{redemption.mean_quality_redeemed_weighted}}) exceeded that of deposited credits ({{redemption.mean_quality_deposited_weighted}}) by 7 points. As a result, BCT's net renewable share increased from 71% to 76% over the pool's lifetime.

The exit followed a type-level Gresham sequence (bad drives out good; Fig. 5b): the most valuable credit types were extracted first, in an order matching off-chain market demand ($\rho$ = $-$0.74, $n$ = 7 types; Supplementary Methods). Before the May 2022 market crash, redemptions were dominated by high-value credits (tonnage-weighted quality 42.0); afterwards, quality dropped to 32.4, a 10-point gap reflecting selective exit while BCT's price still exceeded the off-chain value of premium credits.

Depositors and redeemers are almost entirely distinct populations: only 1.4% of the {{redemption.n_unique_redeemers}} redeemer accounts also appear among the {{selection.n_wallets}} depositor accounts. The top 10 redeemers account for {{redemption.redeemer_top10_share}}% of extracted tonnage, and their transactions occurred in rapid automated bursts (median gap 4.6 seconds between events), consistent with programmatic rather than manual execution.

This dual-margin structure — indiscriminate, architecture-driven entry (99.6% tonnage pass-through) on one side; deliberate, type-specialist extraction on the other — is not predicted by standard adverse-selection models, which assume a single population.

Fifteen of the twenty largest redeemers never deposited anything, and each targeted a single credit type.

Tracing each redeemed credit's immediate destination across the 20 largest extractors shows three pathways: cross-pool transfer to a quality-screened pool (39.2%), immediate retirement (34.6%), and secondary-market sale (17.0%). The cross-pool movement followed a strict, systematic ordering (deposit, redeem, then re-deposit into the screened pool; Supplementary Methods).

Among the 399 accounts that both deposited and redeemed (the 1.4% overlap population), a quality-swap analysis reveals no systematic quality arbitrage (Supplementary Methods). The dual-margin mechanism thus operates through largely distinct account populations connected by the pool's uniform pricing. A first-funder audit of the top 20 accounts per margin found entity-level links for a minority: one shared funding source, direct credit transfers among three accounts, and two dual-margin accounts, together 26% of top-20 deposit and 9% of top-20 redemption tonnage (Supplementary Methods and Supplementary Table 5). The separation is therefore account-level, not entity-level.

### Pool design and asset fate: a within-token cross-pool contrast

A sharper contrast on the same data comes from {{within_token.n_shared_tokens}} credit tokens (1.50 million tonnes) deposited into *both* the unscreened pool (BCT) and the quality-screened pool (NCT), so the same physical credit appears under both designs with quality, vintage, registry, and project type held fixed. These {{within_token.n_shared_tokens}} are the redeemed members of the 31 credits shared by the two pools, so the unscreened-side rate is near-universal (100%) partly by this selection, while the screened-side rate (28.5%) is not symmetrically conditioned; the gap below is therefore best read as an upper bound. Token by token (the unit of inference, $n$ = {{within_token.n_shared_tokens}}), the mean redemption gap is +{{within_token.mean_diff_pp}} percentage points (95% CI [{{within_token.boot_gap_ci_lo_pp}}, {{within_token.boot_gap_ci_hi_pp}}]; Fig. 4), unanimous in direction across every token and every credit type, with three exact tests (sign, permutation, Wilcoxon) returning $p$ = {{within_token.sign_test_p}}. This is a two-pool contrast, not {{within_token.n_shared_tokens}} independent treatments, and shares the two-pool confounds (exposure timing, liquidity, eligibility) that make the pool-level analysis descriptive only (Methods); its scope is also narrow, covering only nature-based credits (renewables are screened out). We therefore read it as *strongly suggestive* of a pool-design effect rather than a proof, though robust: a hidden confounder would have to more than triple the odds of redemption for one credit over its pair to overturn the result (Γ = {{within_token.sensitivity_gamma}}; Supplementary Methods).

**Cross-pool comparison.** The same association appears beyond BCT, though we are careful about what it does and does not show. Recovering per-token credit identity on-chain for five pools spanning two operators (Toucan and C3, a second bridge protocol) and scoring each by our common framework, mean deposit quality rises monotonically with screening strictness, from the two unscreened pools (~29–31) through the two nature-screened pools (~39–40) to the category-allowlist pool (77.9; Supplementary Table 4). We stress that this gradient is in large part definitional: a pool's screen is defined on credit type, and our composite score is itself driven by credit type, so screened pools must score higher on average. It is consistent with a design effect but does not isolate one, because a within-type check confirms the boundary: screened and unscreened pools hold nature-based credits of near-identical within-type quality (differences <=0.3 points; Supplementary Methods), so screening acts on type, not within-type quality; the quantity is framework-derived (the C3 pools are scored only by our rubric, with no external validation). The two unscreened pools additionally show a declining temporal-quality slope (BCT $\rho$ = {{multipool.bct_temporal_rho}}; C3 $\rho$ = {{multipool.c3_temporal_rho}}, $p$ = {{multipool.c3_temporal_p}}) absent in the screened pools, but these raw slopes are not vintage-controlled and may reflect the same vintage-composition drift we identified for BCT above rather than a design-driven decline. We therefore read the cross-pool pattern as corroborative and descriptive, not as independent evidence that design causes quality.


### Quality atlas and quality gating

BCT's PQD of {{quality.bct_pqd}} places it at the bottom of the VCM quality spectrum, which spans a 10-fold range from {{quality.quality_atlas_pqd_min}} (DACCS, direct air carbon capture and storage) to {{quality.quality_atlas_pqd_max}} (grid-connected renewables) (Fig. 3). A within-pool permutation test ($z$ = $-$0.64, $p$ = 0.27) finds no evidence of within-pool selection bias; the eligible universe was uniformly low-quality. Letter grades B–AAA map the composite score to bands; the BBB threshold separates credits with verified additionality from those without. Applied to the actual deposit stream, a BBB gate would have cut the deposit-weighted deficit from {{quality.bbb_gate_baseline}} to {{quality.bbb_gate_pqd}} while admitting only {{quality.bbb_gate_admitted_pct}}% of deposited tonnage (Fig. 6); the Toucan CHAR pool (biochar allowlist, PQD {{quality.char_pqd}}) demonstrates that category restriction prevents quality collapse (Supplementary Fig. 2).


### Temporal dynamics and robustness

Deposit quality declines over BCT's operating life (Spearman $\rho$ = $-$0.24, $n$ = {{composition.n_deposits}}), but this is entirely a vintage-composition effect: late deposits drew from progressively older vintages (vintage, the credit's emission-reduction year), not from intrinsically worse credits within vintage (Supplementary Table 2). The May 2022 cryptocurrency crash provides exogenous deposit-side evidence of the causal channel: post-shock deposit quality fell 3.4 points (Cohen's $d$ = 0.49, a moderate effect) as volume collapsed by 98% and the quality-decline slope tripled, consistent with the price-to-quality feedback above (Supplementary Methods).


### Market-revealed quality hierarchy

Credit type alone, not the quality framework, captures the dominant axis of selective redemption: a framework-free type-only prediction rule out-predicts the quality-grade rule, and within the 116 renewable tokens neither vintage nor grade predicts redemption (Supplementary Table 3). The paper's core findings (composition, base-rate over-selection, redemption-side extraction, and the within-token cross-pool comparison) accordingly rest on transaction data and Verra credit-type classifications, not on the quality scoring framework (Supplementary Table 6).

The bottom line: 9.3 of the 9.6 million deposited tonnes of B-grade credits (the 161-token scored analysis covers 15.2 million tonnes) remain unredeemed, one of the largest carbon credit graveyards in existence.
